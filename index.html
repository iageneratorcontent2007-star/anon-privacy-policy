Anon — Privacy Policy

This Privacy Policy explains how Anon collects, uses, stores, and shares information when you use the Anon application. It is written to match the features that exist in the current product, including Google sign-in, anonymous profiles, text chat, voice and video, matchmaking, translation, coins, VIP, advertising, and safety tools.

1. Introduction

Anon is an anonymous social chat service. You can create an anonymous profile, find other people through matchmaking, send messages, join rooms, and, where available, use voice or video. Anon is operated using backend services hosted by Supabase and, on Android, Google Play services.

“Anon,” “we,” “us,” and “our” refer to the operator of the Anon application. “You” means the person who installs or uses Anon. This policy applies to the Anon mobile application and related clients that connect to the same backend.

Anon is designed so that other users see an anonymous name, avatar, and public user ID, not your Google account name. That does not mean Anon collects no personal data. We collect the information described below to operate the service, prevent abuse, process purchases, show ads, and handle safety reports.

2. Information We Collect

Depending on how you use Anon, we may collect:

  • Account and authentication data from Google sign-in.
  • Profile data that you choose, such as anonymous name, avatar, gender preference, language, and country or region.
  • Internal and public user identifiers.
  • Messages, room activity, friend or heart actions, and related chat metadata.
  • Media files that you choose to upload.
  • Voice and video connection data needed to start and maintain a call.
  • Approximate or precise location if you grant location permission.
  • Matchmaking queue data, selected mode, and region-matching preferences.
  • Translations of messages that you request or that the service generates for a conversation.
  • Coin balances, purchases, VIP entitlements, and related transaction records.
  • Advertising identifiers and ad-performance data when ads are shown.
  • Reports, blocks, permission states, and moderation records.
  • Security signals such as a hashed app-install identifier, a coarse device label, and a hashed IP address.
  • Technical logs needed to operate, debug, and secure the service.

We do not ask for your Google password. We do not use IMEI, MAC addresses, or hardware fingerprints as account identifiers.

3. Account and Profile Information

You can sign in with Google. Anon requests the Google OAuth scopes openid, email, and profile. Google may provide us with your Google account identifier, email address, and basic profile information. Anon uses this to create or recover one Anon account for that Google identity.

Your public profile in Anon is separate from your Google identity. Other users typically see an anonymous name, avatar, online status, and public user ID. They do not see your email address in the normal chat or matchmaking interface.

We may store profile fields that you set, including:

  • Anonymous display name and avatar.
  • Gender and “looking for” preferences used for matching.
  • Preferred language.
  • Country or region code used for regional matchmaking.
  • Account status, such as active, restricted, or banned.

Authorized administrators may access private identity fields such as email for support, safety, and account administration. Those views are audited.

4. User ID

Anon uses more than one identifier:

  • Authentication user ID. A unique account ID created when you sign in. This ID is used by the backend to attach your profile, chats, purchases, and permissions to the correct account.
  • Public user ID. A visible identifier in the form of a short public code, such as an ANON- ID. You and other users, and administrators, can use this ID to recognize an account without sharing your email.
  • Google subject / provider ID. Used so that one Google identity maps to one Anon account.

The public ID is part of your in-app identity. The authentication ID and Google identity are not shown to other regular users as a Google email or Google account name.

5. Messages and Communications

When you send a text message in a conversation or room, Anon stores the message content and related metadata on our backend. This typically includes the sender, conversation or room, timestamp, and message text.

If you upload an image or other file, we store the file in application storage and keep metadata such as owner, storage path, type, size, and the related conversation or room.

Temporary match chats, private chats after a friend connection, and room messages are communications on the service. We do not claim that messages are never stored. Stored messages may be used to deliver the chat, show history, generate translations, and review safety reports.

Other participants in a conversation or room can see the content you send them. Authorized administrators may review messages when needed for moderation, abuse investigation, or legal compliance. Those reviews can be logged.

6. Voice and Audio

Voice chat and voice matchmaking use the device microphone and WebRTC to send audio between participants. To connect the call, Anon exchanges signaling data through our backend and may use a STUN service, including Google’s public STUN servers, to help devices find a network path.

Anon is not operated as a call-recording product. The live audio stream is used to provide the call. We still process signaling, session, matchmaking, permission, and safety data related to the voice session. Network providers and STUN services may process technical connection data.

Voice features may require an active VIP entitlement. The entitlement check is performed by our servers, not only on the device.

7. Video and Camera

Video chat and video matchmaking use the camera and microphone. Anon accesses the camera only after the relevant permission is granted and you start a video feature. You can turn the camera off during a call in the call controls.

Video is transmitted with WebRTC in the same way as voice. We do not claim that video never leaves your device: it is sent to the other participant so the call can work. Signaling and session records are processed on our backend. Authorized administrators may review related session or report information.

Video features may also require an active VIP entitlement, which is checked on the server.

8. Microphone Permissions

Anon requests microphone permission when you use voice or video. On Android this corresponds to the microphone / record-audio permission. The app uses the microphone to capture audio for the active call or voice session.

If you deny microphone permission, voice and video features that need audio will not work normally. You can change the permission later in the device settings or in the in-app privacy/permissions screen.

Anon may also request related permissions when you use the corresponding feature: camera for video, photos for uploads, notifications for alerts, and location for location features.

9. Location / Region Information

Region or country you select

You may set a country or region code on your profile. Anon uses this for regional matchmaking and related eligibility checks. This is profile information that you choose. It is not the same as GPS location.

Device location

If you grant location permission, Anon may read your device location and submit latitude, longitude, and accuracy to our backend. Location is used for location-based features and may be reviewed by authorized administrators for safety or support. Location is not required to create an account.

We do not treat location as a public profile field shown to every other user. Granting location is optional. You can refuse or later revoke the permission.

10. Matchmaking

When you search for someone, Anon places you in a matchmaking queue. We process the selected mode (text, voice, or video), your profile preferences, online status, and, if you choose regional matching, your country or region code.

A successful match creates a conversation or session between you and another account. We store the match/session records needed to connect you, apply coin or VIP rules, and keep the chat or call working.

Regional matching and voice/video matching may require VIP or other existing eligibility rules. Those checks are enforced on the server.

11. Automatic Translation

Anon can translate chat messages into a target language, using your preferred language where available. To do this, message text is sent from the app to our server-side translation service.

The translation service may then send that text to a third-party translation provider. Depending on server configuration, this may include Google Cloud Translation or another translation API. The provider returns a translated text and may detect a source language. Anon may store the translation so it does not have to be requested again for the same message and language.

Do not send information in chat that you are not willing to have processed for delivery, storage, moderation, or translation.

12. Coins and Virtual Currency

Anon uses a virtual currency called Coins. Coins can be received through existing in-app actions such as purchases, rewarded ads, daily bonuses, or administrator adjustments. Coins can be spent on existing features such as certain matchmaking or room actions, according to the current economy rules.

We store your coin balance and a history of economy transactions. Coins are a limited license to use in-app features. They are not real-world money, and they have no cash value except where a store or applicable law requires otherwise.

13. VIP

VIP is a time-limited entitlement. It can be activated when you complete an eligible Google Play purchase, or when an authorized administrator grants VIP. The backend stores VIP status and an expiration time.

While VIP is active, existing VIP features such as voice and video access and regional matching are available according to the current product rules. When VIP expires or is revoked, those features are limited again.

We store entitlement records so the app and the server can check whether VIP is currently active. We do not treat an old purchase by itself as proof that VIP is still active after it has expired.

14. Google Play purchases

On Android, coin packs and VIP subscriptions are sold through Google Play Billing. Google processes the payment. Anon receives purchase confirmation data needed to credit coins or activate VIP, such as a product identifier and a purchase token. We store purchase and entitlement records to fulfill the order and prevent duplicate credit.

Google may collect payment method details, purchase history, and account information under Google’s own policies. Anon does not receive your full payment-card number through the app.

Refunds, billing disputes, and subscription cancellation for Play purchases are handled under Google Play policies and any applicable consumer law.

15. Advertising

Anon uses Google AdMob to show advertising, including banner ads and rewarded ads. Rewarded ads may grant Coins after the ad is completed and verified. Coin credit for rewarded ads is confirmed on the server after AdMob server-side verification, not by a coin amount typed on the device.

AdMob and Google may collect device and advertising identifiers, approximate location derived by Google, IP address, interaction data, and similar technical information to deliver, measure, and combat fraud in ads. This processing is also governed by Google’s privacy policy and ad settings.

You may be able to reset or limit advertising identifiers in your device settings. Doing so can affect ad personalization; it does not remove the need for ads or for the technical data required to show them.

16. Moderation, Reports and Safety

You can block another user and submit a report with a reason and, where relevant, the related conversation. We store blocks and reports so we can hide or limit contact and review possible abuse.

Authorized administrators can review accounts, messages, uploaded media metadata, location records, economy data, and reports. They may restrict, ban, or otherwise act on an account. Admin actions and sensitive views can be written to audit logs.

We may also keep security events, rate-limit records, and risk signals to reduce spam, multi-accounting, and attacks. Hashed IP addresses and hashed install identifiers are used for these safety purposes. A hashed IP is not shown on your public profile and is not used as a permanent account lock by itself.

17. Test / Bot Accounts

Anon includes test or bot accounts in the production codebase. These accounts are used for quality testing, development, and administrator verification of matchmaking, chat, and call flows. They are marked as test-bot accounts in our systems and are not ordinary user accounts.

Regular matchmaking is designed to pair real users with other real users. Test bots are used when that testing feature is enabled by administrators. If you are matched with a test account during testing, the conversation is still processed as chat or call data on our systems.

Test-bot accounts are not a substitute for human moderation and are not used to collect extra personal data beyond the session needed to test the product.

18. How We Use Information

We use the information described in this policy to:

  • Create and maintain your account and anonymous profile.
  • Sign you in with Google and keep one Google identity linked to one Anon account.
  • Operate matchmaking, chats, rooms, friends, and voice or video sessions.
  • Translate messages when that feature is used.
  • Apply coin, VIP, and purchase rules.
  • Show ads and grant verified ad rewards.
  • Provide customer support and administrator tools.
  • Detect, investigate, and prevent fraud, spam, abuse, and security incidents.
  • Comply with law, enforce our terms, and protect users and the service.
  • Improve reliability and understand how features are used, using operational metrics rather than selling a browse history of your chats.

19. How We Share Information

We do not sell your chat contents as a data product. We do share information in these cases:

  • Other users. People you match with, chat with, or join in a room can see your public profile and the content you send them.
  • Service providers. We use companies that process data on our behalf or as independent controllers for their services, including:
    • Supabase, for authentication, database, file storage, realtime features, and server functions.
    • Google, for sign-in, Play Billing, AdMob, STUN connectivity, and, when configured, Cloud Translation.
    • Other translation APIs if they are configured on the translation service.
  • Administrators. Authorized operators may access account and safety data to run the service.
  • Legal and safety. We may disclose information if we believe it is reasonably necessary to comply with law, legal process, or to protect users, the public, or Anon.
  • Business changes. If the service is transferred, relevant data may be transferred with it, subject to this policy or a successor notice.

Those third parties process information under their own privacy policies as well as any agreement they have with us. You can read Google’s policy at policies.google.com/privacy and Supabase’s policy at supabase.com/privacy.

20. Data Retention

We keep information for as long as needed to provide Anon and for the purposes in this policy.

  • Account, profile, public ID, and linked Google identity: while the account exists.
  • Messages, media metadata, translations, friends, blocks, and match/session records: while needed to operate chats and rooms, and longer if required for safety, disputes, or law.
  • Location records: until deleted with the account or removed in the course of normal operations.
  • Coin, purchase, and VIP records: while needed to fulfill purchases, prevent fraud, and keep entitlements accurate.
  • Reports, bans, and admin audit logs: while needed for safety and operational review.
  • Hashed IP addresses, hashed install identifiers, and routine security events: generally about 30 days, then purged by the security-retention process, unless a longer period is needed for an investigation.

Google, AdMob, and other providers may retain their own logs under their policies, which we do not control.

21. Account and Data Deletion

You can delete your Anon account yourself from Profile, Settings, or Settings → Privacy & Permissions. The app shows a warning and asks for confirmation. After you confirm, the server deletes the personal application data listed on the Delete Account page and signs you out.

Deleted data includes your profile, public user ID, private identity, messages you sent, uploaded media, location records, friends and blocks, rooms you own, coins, VIP, sessions, and the Anon sign-in user when the server can remove it.

Some records may remain without your public profile: safety reports and incidents, administrator audit logs, purchase and rewarded-ad receipts needed for billing or fraud checks, hashed security signals for about 30 days, operational backups for a limited time, and copies another person may still have on their own device. Deleting Anon does not delete your Google account. Play purchase history remains with Google.

Step-by-step instructions are published here: https://iageneratorcontent2007-star.github.io/anon-privacy-policy/delete-account.html. You can also contact nikoparravicini34@gmail.com.

22. Security

We use technical and organizational measures appropriate to the nature of the service. These include encrypted transport (HTTPS), authenticated API access, server-side authorization for admin and entitlement actions, and access controls on application data. Sensitive administrator actions can be audited.

No method of transmission or storage is completely secure. You should not share information in Anon that you cannot afford to have seen by another participant or, in a safety investigation, by an administrator.

Anon does not store your Google password. Do not send passwords, backup codes, or payment-card numbers in chat.

23. Children's Privacy

Anon is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. You must also be old enough to use Google and this app in your country.

If you believe a child under 13 has created an Anon account, contact nikoparravicini34@gmail.com. We will take reasonable steps to delete the account and related information.

24. International Users

Anon may be used from different countries. Our service providers, including Supabase and Google, may process information in the United States and other countries where they operate. Those countries may have data-protection laws that differ from the laws in your country.

By using Anon, you understand that your information may be transferred to and processed in those locations as needed to provide the service.

If a change is material, we will take reasonable steps to provide notice, for example by updating this page, noting the change in the app listing, or showing a notice in the app. The current version of this page is the policy that applies.
