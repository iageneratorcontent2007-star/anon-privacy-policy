A Anon Delete Account Privacy Policy

Delete your Anon account

This page explains how to delete an Anon account yourself, what Anon removes, and what may be kept for safety or billing. It matches the current Anon application and backend.

App name

Anon is an anonymous chat, matchmaking, and voice/video application.

How you can delete your account

A signed-in user can delete their own account from the Anon app. The request is sent to Anon’s servers. After a successful deletion, the app signs you out and clears the local session.

You can also email nikoparravicini34@gmail.com if you cannot open the app and need help deleting the account.

Step-by-step instructions

  1. Open the Anon app and sign in with Google if you are not already signed in.
  2. Open Profile, or open Settings from your profile.
  3. You can also open Settings → Privacy & Permissions.
  4. Tap Delete account.
  5. Read the warning. It explains that deletion is permanent.
  6. Tap Delete permanently to confirm, or Cancel to keep the account.
  7. When deletion succeeds, Anon signs you out. The next Google sign-in creates a new empty Anon profile, not the old one.

Data that is deleted

When you confirm deletion, Anon removes the personal application data tied to that account, including:

  • Your Anon profile: anonymous name, avatar, public user ID, gender and looking-for preferences, language, and country or region.
  • Private identity stored for the account, such as the linked Google email used to operate sign-in.
  • Messages you sent, including stored translations of those messages.
  • Media files you uploaded and their metadata.
  • Location points stored after you granted location permission.
  • Permission-state records, sessions, and matchmaking queue entries.
  • Friends, hearts, friend requests, and blocks created by or against your account.
  • Rooms you own, and your membership in other rooms.
  • Coins, wallet balance, VIP entitlements, and in-app economy history for that account.
  • The Anon authentication user, when the server is able to remove it, so the old account cannot be reused.
  • Local session and profile data on the device after sign-out.

Data that may be retained

Anon does not keep your public profile after deletion. Some records may remain without that profile because they are needed for safety, billing, or security:

  • Safety reports and incidents. The report reason, time, and status may remain. The link to your user ID is removed.
  • Administrator audit logs. Actions already recorded for safety or support may remain.
  • Purchase receipts. A Play purchase token, product ID, and time may remain so Anon can prevent duplicate credit, handle refunds, and investigate fraud. Your public profile is not kept on that receipt. Google also keeps Play purchase history under Google’s own policy.
  • Rewarded-ad receipts. A reward identifier and time may remain to prevent repeated credit of the same ad reward.
  • Hashed security signals. Hashed IP or install signals and security events may remain for about 30 days, then they are purged by the existing security-retention process, unless a longer review is required for an active investigation.
  • Your Google account. Deleting Anon does not delete the Google account you used to sign in.
  • Copies on other devices. Another person may still have a copy of a message you already sent to them on their own device.
  • Backups. Operational backups may hold residual data for a limited time until they rotate.

Retention periods for kept data

  • Hashed security events: about 30 days, then automatic purge.
  • Reports, incidents, and admin audit logs: while needed for safety, abuse review, or legal compliance.
  • Purchase and rewarded-ad receipts: while needed for billing, refunds, chargebacks, or fraud prevention.
  • Google Play and Google Ads records: retained by Google under Google’s policies, which Anon does not control.
